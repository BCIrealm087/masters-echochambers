# Echo chambers in Brazil’s 2022 presidential election

This Computer Science master’s project studies whether groups of accounts found in Twitter data collected by Interfaces during Brazil’s 2022 presidential election continue to amplify sources within their own group in a later period. The source is the Interfaces Twitter Elections Dataset (ITED-Br). The earlier manuscript reports 282 million posts in the parent collection. Each analysis in this project will describe the subset it actually uses.

The structural pilot asks how much internal reposting persists after accounting for sharer activity, original-post popularity, day and collection context. Independent evidence about political alignment, the material amplified and the alternatives available to users determines how far an echo-chamber interpretation can extend. The project also asks what circulates within and across these communities. For example, do groups use different language to support one candidate and attack another? Which sources circulate within a group, and which cross its boundaries? Questions about belief and trust will require an explicit account of what the observed behavior can support.

The first empirical milestone is the [October repost coverage audit](docs/reports/m2-repost-coverage-audit.md). It measures how many distinct reposts from 16–21 October 2022 can be dated and linked to both the sharing account and the historical original author. Its day-by-query coverage table determines whether the planned comparison across time is feasible. The selected language follow-up asks whether criticism of Lula or Bolsonaro appeared across many members’ own writing or gained visibility through repeated sharing of a few original posts.
