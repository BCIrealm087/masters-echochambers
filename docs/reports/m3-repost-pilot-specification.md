# Do frozen account groups explain later reposting?

**M3 · Within-corpus repost pilot · Protocol v1 · 2026-11-26**

Can account groups learned from 16–18 October 2022 explain how recorded reposts were allocated on 19–21 October after accounting for activity, original-post popularity and familiar sharer–author ties? M2 recovered both endpoints for 99.760% of verified actions. The pilot will estimate excess internal sharing under a matched reference and separately measure predictive gain beyond tie history. This protocol fixes the analysis rules. The discovery partition and executable run manifest will be frozen after discovery checks and before any holdout outcome is scored. [M2 audit](m2-repost-coverage-audit.md) · [Statistical design](reflexive-report-5.md) · [Discovery design](reflexive-report-6.md)

## Population and graph

Use the M2 host snapshot and reconciliation rules. Assign actions by São Paulo date and deduplicate by action ID. Preserve Lula only, Bolsonaro only and both as disjoint collection strata. Exclude verified self-reposts from discovery and the primary holdout comparison. Retain their counts and unresolved references by day and context. Unknown author remains a missing endpoint. For the holdout estimand, keep resolved authors outside the frozen partition as external and report actions from unassigned sharers in the coverage ledger.

An account is a node. A directed arc runs from a sharer to the original author and its weight is their number of distinct eligible discovery actions. Use all incident vertices and all non-self arcs in the primary graph, including disconnected components. Keep binary weights on the same arcs as a sensitivity. Use later partitions only for historical identity recovery. Compute graph features, weights and discovery rankings from discovery actions.

## Choose and freeze the discovery partition

1. Verify both optimizers against the [three-node directed fixture](reflexive-report-6.md#discovery-benchmark-and-freeze), including objective normalization, stored direction and strengths. Prefer directed weighted Leiden with the RB configuration objective and iterate until the implementation reports convergence. Use verified directed Louvain as a fallback if Leiden fails the fixture or the full discovery grid cannot run within the host's available resources. Record the failure and rerun the same grid. If neither passes, stop before holdout. Pin package versions and thread settings in the implementation manifest before fitting.
2. Run γ in {0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2}, seeds 0–19, using stable vertex and arc ordering. A run is eligible if every group induces a weakly connected subgraph and at least two groups each contain 20 distinct discovery sharers and account for at least 100 discovery actions made by those sharers. Check these counts before any holdout visibility. A resolution is eligible if at least 10 runs pass. Maximize the median pairwise adjusted Rand index among eligible runs at a resolution. Break ties within 0.000001 by proximity to γ = 1 and then smaller γ. Use the eligible run with the highest mean adjusted Rand index to the other eligible runs, breaking ties within 0.000001 by smaller seed. If no resolution is eligible, report discovery feasibility and keep the holdout sealed.
3. Record variation of information, adjusted mutual information, adjusted Rand index, matched group Jaccard overlap, sizes, connectivity and sharer and action coverage. Benchmark the other verified modularity optimizer on the same graph and grid. Apply the rule separately to the binary graph. At the primary resolution and seed refit after omitting each discovery day and after removing the top 0.01%, 0.05% and 0.1% of vertices by discovery in-strength. Rank positive in-strength vertices, round counts up and break ties by numeric account ID. Compare directed Infomap on weighted and binary graphs with 20 trials, fixed seed 20 and unrecorded teleportation at 0.15. Use the flat partition for the matched comparison and record hierarchy and a reversed-arc sensitivity. Register any secondary run that fails or exceeds resources.
4. Freeze the exact eligible-action rules, graph and partition hashes, methods, settings, scores, sensitivity memberships, prediction configuration and inference implementation revision in a dated manifest. Keep memberships, account and post identifiers, and intermediate ledgers local. Archive shareable counts, hashes and diagnostics. A choice informed by holdout outcomes requires a fresh confirmation interval.

## Holdout inference

The primary population is verified non-self reposts made by assigned sharers with resolved authors and known query provenance. Score an action as internal if its sharer and author share a frozen group. Use one event-weighted global contrast, observed internal share minus mean reference share, in percentage points.

Within each local day × disjoint context, permute original-post slots among the observed action slots, preserving each sharer's total activity and each original's total number of reposts. Keep the action timestamp at its slot. Exclude pairings to one's own post and to originals published after the slot time using the original ID clock at whole-second precision. Check the legality of observed pairings and document the timestamp and repeated sharer–post semantics before running inference. Treat exact same-second publication as feasible. Keep multiplicity where the ledger records repeated actions. If a structural uniqueness rule is independently verified, encode it and validate the resulting state space before inference. Compare pooled and day-only references on the same eligible actions.

Use uniform exact sampling where tractable. For constrained strata, validate the target distribution on exhaustively enumerable examples, including disconnected swap spaces. If using reversible swaps, retain rejected proposals as self-transitions and validate detailed balance, reachability, margin preservation and mixing from dispersed starts. Include immobile strata at their fixed contribution. If usable sampling or a valid analytic calculation cannot be established, report observed counts and feasible reference diagnostics and withhold the primary p-value. Unrestricted permutations may be reported as a separately labeled sensitivity. Use 9,999 independent draws where available and report the upper-tail Monte Carlo p-value with the plus-one correction. For an approved correlated sampler report the tail estimate and Monte Carlo uncertainty based on effective simulation size. Before opening outcomes, simulate null and injected-affinity ledgers using discovery inputs, with roughly 1,000 replicates per scenario. Include viral originals, repeated ties, heterogeneous activity and group-selective loss.

Use one-sided α = 0.05 and the registered provisional +5 percentage-point practical screen, with +2.5, +10 and +20 point sensitivities. Report observed and expected shares, the central 95% null range and uncertainty separately. Apply Holm to the secondary family of frozen groups with at least 100 eligible actions and 20 eligible distinct sharers, with eligibility based on margins alone. Report all group dispositions.

## Prediction beyond prior ties

For each eligible holdout action, score the historical author conditional on the observed sharer, timestamp and query provenance. Use a shared risk set of all authors observed in discovery or among the resolved holdout destinations in that day and query, excluding the sharer. The holdout support is a conditional evaluation choice. It exposes which authors appear in the stratum and defines a retrospective choice task. Training weights use discovery counts only. Report a discovery-only support sensitivity with unseen authors mapped to one external category that receives the same uniform smoothing as a known author.

Compare nested author distributions. Start with smoothed discovery author popularity, add context-specific popularity, then add a smoothed sharer–author history component based on frequency and recency. For the final model multiply the history probabilities by exp(β) when sharer and author share a frozen group, and renormalize over the same authors. Use exactly the same fitted history probabilities in the last two models. Add one total pseudo-count spread uniformly across each risk set for global popularity and use that distribution as the context backoff prior. Back off dyad history to the smoothed context distribution. Pool a sharer’s actions across contexts for the dyad counts, weighting each by 2 raised to minus its age divided by the half-life. Normalize every model on the common risk set. Fit context smoothing first, dyad smoothing and half-life second, and β third, holding earlier choices fixed. Tune the context and dyad prior masses from {1, 10, 100}, history half-life from {1 day, 3 days, infinite}, and β from {−2, −1, −0.5, 0, 0.5, 1, 2} by maximum aggregate log score in rolling discovery folds. Fit on October 16 and validate on October 17, then fit on October 16–17 and validate on October 18. Validate using the same risk-set construction on fold data. Infer each fold's groups from its training prefix using the discovery rule. If only one fold supplies eligible groups, use that fold for β. Score common membership as false when either fold account lacks a group. If no fold supplies an eligible partition, set β = 0 and report the history comparison as uncalibrated for incremental membership. Break ties by larger backoff mass, longer half-life and β closest to zero, then smaller β. Recompute counts through October 18 and freeze the final model. Evaluate mean paired log-score gain in nats per action and by day, query and dyads unseen in discovery. Report sharer-clustered uncertainty conditional on the window and assess sensitivity to dependence on popular authors and posts.

## Missingness, concentration and decisions

Report coverage by group, day, context, sharer activity and target reuse, including an explicit count for authors outside the frozen partition. Recompute the raw internal-share bounds among assigned sharers by setting all otherwise eligible unknown destinations external and internal. In the matched-reference sensitivity, impute unknown authors once per post, comparing assignments toward internal or external groups and toward groups of frequent versus rare sources. Respect known eligibility constraints and recompute the reference for each completion. For discovery, rerun the frozen partition procedure under corresponding endpoint completions and pair each refit with its holdout sensitivity.

Compare the primary estimate with equal-sharer and distinct-dyad weighting, applying the same statistic to observed and permuted allocations. Also report leave-one-day-out and per-context estimates, and estimates after removing the top one, five and 1% of posts and authors by holdout frequency. Recompute the eligible margins after each removal. Report maximum shares and inverse squared-share sums for sharers, authors, posts and dyads. Report previously observed versus new dyads and separate newly observed originals from originals with verified newly published dates. If conclusions hinge on one source or a coverage scenario, narrow them accordingly. A positive structural contrast supports persistent internal amplification in this observation frame. Positive incremental held-out log score supports added predictive information beyond tie history. Political alignment, exposure and trust each require separate evidence.
