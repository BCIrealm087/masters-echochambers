# Search and evidence notes for echo-chamber detection

**M1 · Step 3 supporting notes · 2026-11-24**

These notes document the search for Q1 from the [M1 review protocol](m1-review-protocol.md). The question concerns definitions, automated group detection, group membership, and observed circulation. The [Step 3 report](m1-echo-chamber-detection.md) gives the implications for ITED-Br.

## Search record

The focused pass used 22 scholarly web queries on 2026-11-24, requesting up to five results per query. All 110 returned result entries received an initial title or snippet screen. The count includes duplicates across queries. The smaller depth per query is a departure from the Step 1 starting limit of 20. This pass spread retrieval across more terminology variants for the same research question, then used targeted citation lookups for close precedents and contrasting findings. There was no date filter. Domain constraints are written into the queries where used. Fifteen distinct references informed the synthesis or its methodological interpretation, along with the ITED-Br documentation already inventoried in Step 2. Dinkelberg et al. and Mahmoudi et al. were kept as bibliographic leads. This scope is appropriate to a focused review supporting a pilot proposal. It leaves the wider search coverage open.

## Citation tracing

- Backward from Terren and Borge-Bravo’s reference list identified Barberá et al., whose topic-specific comparison qualifies claims that interaction is always segregated. Forward tracing identified Cooper et al., whose survey-based measure addresses political awareness and agreement. This difference in evidence helps define what a structural ITED-Br analysis could claim. [Terren and Borge-Bravo](https://rcommunicationr.org/index.php/rcr/article/view/16) · [Barberá et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797615594620) · [Cooper et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0324507)
- Backward from Interian and Rodrigues found Cota et al. as an earlier Brazilian network and diffusion comparator. A targeted search for citing or related work found overlapping 2022 election graph studies, including the descriptive candidate endorsement study by Dinkelberg, Santana and Bazzan. The latter is a bibliographic lead while its full methods are unverified. [Interian and Rodrigues](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-072X/acf6a4) · [Cota et al.](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-019-0213-9) · [Dinkelberg et al. institutional record](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/endorsement-networks-in-the-2022-brazilian-presidential-elections/)

## Evidence extracted

| Source | Population, measure and evidence | Relevance and limitation for ITED-Br |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Terren and Borge-Bravo, 2021](https://rcommunicationr.org/index.php/rcr/article/view/16) | Review of 55 social-media studies. Compares design and evidence from behavioral traces and self-reports. Conclusions vary with the evidence source and operational definition. | Use the literature to define observable criteria. Avoid asserting that a result measured from interactions describes the full media diet. |
| [Nguyen, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/epi.2018.32) | Conceptual account of epistemic exclusion. The stronger notion involves outsiders’ credibility being actively undermined. | A separate claim about expressed trust or discrediting needs contextual evidence. No behavioral effect size is implied. |
| [Interian and Rodrigues, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-072X/acf6a4) | Portuguese election-keyword Twitter sample, about 15.2 million posts, 17 September–30 October 2022. Directed account repost network. Multilevel partition on 17 September–29 October. Compares communities’ modularity contributions and potential network reach. | Direct event and platform comparator. News media formed a large third group. Study window, keyword coverage, inclusion of election day and the validation target differ from the proposed ITED-Br design. |
| [Ferreira et al.](https://doi.org/10.26694/2317-3254.rcp.v13i2.6933) | Portuguese Twitter discussion about the Brazilian electoral system in 2022, 25 September–8 October. Automated grouping followed by qualitative analysis by three coders on the top 50 posts by repost count per selected group. | Relevant local example for examining what circulated most visibly. The sample estimates prominent discourse in that topic, with limited transfer to the prevalence of discourse across all group posts. |
| [Conover et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v5i1.14126) | More than 250,000 U.S. Twitter posts around the 2010 midterms. Compares directed repost and mention networks, including replies in the mention layer. Uses manual political labels and a content comparison. Finds stronger political separation in reposts. | Keep interaction modes distinct. Cross-group mentions can be adversarial. Focus on the largest components and event-specific sample limits transfer of numeric effects. |
| [Barberá et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797615594620) | About 150 million U.S. Twitter posts across 12 political and nonpolitical topics. Compares retweet patterns using separately estimated user ideology based on following behavior. Reports segregation for several political topics and more cross-ideological communication for other events. | Reasons to hold topic and window explicit and seek labels from a distinct signal. Public ITED-Br does not provide the follower edges required to reproduce that stance method. The publisher abstract and authors’ research summary inform this comparison. |
| [Cinelli et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2023301118) | Over 100 million content items across Gab, Facebook, Reddit and Twitter. Compares leaning similarity in interaction networks and the polarity of modeled or observed spreading, depending on platform. | Gives distinct structural and diffusion dimensions to test. Platform and topic differences limit direct comparison. Evidence from a model should be treated as conditional on that model. |
| [Garimella et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3178876.3186139) | Twitter data across political and comparison topics. Compares the polarity of shared URLs with content from followed accounts as a proxy for what could be consumed. Considers accounts that share partisan content while following diverse sources. | Motivates keeping production, reposting and potential exposure separate. The original method’s follower graph and URL evidence differ from the public ITED-Br release. |
| [Cota et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-019-0213-9) | About 12 million Twitter messages around the Rousseff impeachment. Computes hashtag-based leanings and models epidemic-style spreading over a network, focusing on a strongly connected component and checking alternate hashtag classifications. | Brazilian precedent for linking stance, topology and diffusion. Its simulated diffusion and component selection limit comparison to observed circulation across the released graph. |
| [Cooper et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0324507) | Survey/game measuring political opinions and participants’ predictions of population opinion. Reports systematic differences in political awareness without establishing a general echo-chamber explanation for perception biases. | Counterpoint based on a different unit and measurement strategy. The test concerns awareness and belief inference, beyond what ITED-Br reposts directly observe. |
| [Migliorini et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10115-023-01878-7) | Develops constraints for inferring possible social provenance paths from Twitter traces and illustrates them with a real dataset. Repost observations give origin references while intermediate forwarding is unobserved. | Keep original-post reach and observed reference chains distinct from inferred transmission trees. An inferred path would need its own validation. |
| [Brazil–Canada news-media comparison, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-022-00357-3) | Trending-hashtag samples from Brazil 2018 and Canada 2019. Weekly weighted retweet graphs, political orientation estimated from hashtags, and news engagement around bridge accounts. | Cross-group engagement with a news domain can coexist with partisan selection. Topic selection and repost-as-endorsement assumptions constrain interpretation. |

The analytical design also draws on three methodological references. The papers are used for their methods, with no transfer of empirical effect sizes.

- Degree-constrained graph models depend on the chosen graph representation and whether the graph allows parallel events or self-links. The ITED-Br comparison should preserve event-level activity before any simple-graph sensitivity check. [Fosdick et al.](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1087175) · [Hobson et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12775)
- Repeated partitions can quantify co-assignment. A dense consensus matrix can grow much larger than a sparse graph, so a pilot can start with aligned group membership across repeated runs. [Lancichinetti and Fortunato](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep00336)
