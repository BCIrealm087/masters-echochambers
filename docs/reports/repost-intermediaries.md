# Can an internal repost follow contact outside the group?

**Circulation follow-up to M3 · Literature review and measurement protocol v1 · 2026-11-27**

When one account reposts an author in its own community, could discussion of that very post have crossed the community boundary first? ITED-Br can potentially document some such cases. Consider an outside account B that quotes a post by A. C replies to B’s quote and subsequently reposts A’s original. If A and C share a frozen group, the native repost is internal and the earlier exchange crosses its boundary. Both statements can be true of a single action. The reason C chose to repost and the screen from which C did so remain unknown.

We adopt a secondary study of **documented same-item contact before reposting**. Its contribution is to measure how often the archive can establish that these two behaviors coexist, for which groups and items, and how much of the activity it leaves unresolved. This complements the distinct-original overlap study. M3 measures later amplification of original authors and predicts the author of a recorded repost. Its registered partition, reference model, prediction targets and holdout decisions use that definition. The present protocol starts with a discovery-only feasibility audit. The query has been checked on fictional records. Empirical contact coverage is open. [M3 protocol](m3-repost-pilot-specification.md) · [Item circulation study](reflexive-report-3.md) · [Research progress](../../STATUS.md)

## What other studies could observe

This focused methods review followed references in studies of retweet provenance and searched for *time-inferred diffusion*, *retweet parent ground truth*, *quote and reply provenance*, *cross-community cascades* and *political network sampling*. We selected original studies that identify the candidate sources, describe an observable against which attribution could be checked, or show how those choices affect a political conclusion. The relevant comparison is the information available at the time of a share. A study can measure group classification or story prediction accurately while the accuracy of its reconstructed paths remains unknown.

| Evidence and study | How the method worked and what transfers |
| --- | --- |
| Twitter activity and follower pairs. Goel, Anderson, Hofman & Watts (2016), Appendix C | The authors combined Firehose activity, a follower graph, explicit reposts and credit conventions. Applying a most-recent-introduction rule to official reposts gave 79% agreement with the parents they considered identifiable under their timeline model. Their 95% overall figure is an estimate combining several classes of adoption. Neither number measures timestamp-only attribution in a keyword sample. The useful baseline ranks earlier **eligible** sources and allows an unknown source. ## Which records answer which question?

Let P be the exact post by A referenced by a native repost R from C. In M3 the directed account edge is C → A. Let Q be a quote of P authored by a third account B, and H be a reply by C to Q. With only reposts from B and C pointing to P, timestamps can exclude a later B. Earlier timing is compatible with both accounts independently encountering P during the same debate or campaign burst. Timing alone supplies no observation connecting C to B. An empty candidate set leaves the route unknown, even when A is the only visible source.

| Observable | Quantity it supports |
| --- | --- |
| R explicitly references P | Amplification by C of the author of P. P is the immediate item referenced by the native repost and can itself contain quoted material. |
| Q quotes P, H replies directly to Q, and C later reposts P | Prior interaction by C with B’s rendering of this exact item. B is a documented contact. The reply can concern B’s commentary. C may already know P or later encounter it elsewhere. |
| C reposts **Q**, and Q explicitly quotes P | A separate descriptive measure of amplification of B’s framing of A’s post. The two content references are observable. Count C → B in the author graph, and record A as the embedded author. Give this analysis its own denominator of native reposts of verified quote posts, showing each lookup loss. |
| B previously reposted P and C had earlier interactions with B on other items | A candidate route under a social-tie hypothesis. Multiple eligible B accounts, A directly, and other unobserved sources remain possible. A shared hashtag, political vocabulary or similar text can motivate content analysis. It cannot establish an item or a contact identity. |

Preserve the disjoint Lula-only, Bolsonaro-only and both-query memberships of each focal repost. Use the archived, frozen primary account groups for descriptive tabulation. 2. **Establish what each ID means.** Reconcile copies by action ID, retaining conflict and missing-value counts. Use the verified whole UTC second from the stored action clock and its ID, following M2. Check publication time for P and Q. Start with the unambiguous single-target types `retweeted`, `quoted` and `replied_to`. The archive also contains `quoted, replied_to` and comma-separated references. Exclude these combined rows from the strict matcher, count them by role, and admit them to a separate sensitivity only if the mapping from each type to its target can be verified. `conversation_id` identifies a thread and cannot identify the direct reply target. [Host inventory](m2-repost-coverage-audit.md#host-inventory-completed-2026-11-26)
3. **Link the actions.** In the four-folder frame collect prior replies from the focal sharers and resolve each exact target Q to its historical ID, author, type, quoted item P and time. Check historical records across the audited archive for immutable post facts. An older Q may be recovered from a later file. Require three distinct accounts, the same C on H and R, identical P on Q and R, and strict order at the verified resolution, `P < Q < H < R`. Equal seconds provide indeterminate order. Keep their counts and flag conflicts, absent parents, unresolved authors, non-quote targets and unsupported types. Construct matches before attaching any group or stance labels.
4. **Define recency and boundaries.** The primary reporting window is `0 < time(R) − time(H) ≤ 6 hours`, with fixed 1-hour and 24-hour sensitivities. These are design choices to measure dependence on the definition of “prior”. The restriction applies to C’s reply. B’s quote can be older. Scan a 24-hour reply buffer before the first focal day where the same collection frame is available. Flag incomplete administrative lookback and record collection gaps by context. A complete scan of the specified window still permits uncollected activity. Show the three windows on their common support as well as the coverage each permits.
5. **Reconcile the denominator on the host.** M2 counted 2,661,101 replies, 427,836 quotes and 34,686 combined actions over the **six** pilot days. It recovered both accounts for 99.760% of verified reposts. Those are single-action counts and endpoint coverage. The multi-step joins above have never been measured in this project. The audited account tables contain follower counts. A historical table of who followed whom and a feed or referral log have yet to be identified. Record per-stage counts by day and query, distinguishing lookup loss from genuine failure to satisfy a rule. Report whether additional archived contexts recover more quotes or replies under a separately identified search frame. ## Counts, comparisons and interpretation

The primary quantity is **X/D**. **D** counts distinct, verified, non-self focal reposts for which C and A belong to the same known frozen group and the full six-hour reply window was searched. **X** counts those events with at least one qualifying B assigned to a different frozen group from C. Every focal repost counts once, however many people C addressed. Include unmatched events in D. Show the number of internal reposts before the lookback restriction, the number lost to that restriction, and the full ledger of between-group, unresolved and unassigned endpoints. The companion distinguishes two known groups to call a crossing. An unassigned B could belong on either side.

Also give the fraction of D with any qualifying contact. Let K count events in D with at least one classifiable B. Report coverage K/D alongside X/K, the outside-contact rate in that selected subset. Partition D into no observed contact, only unassigned contacts, known inside only, known outside only, and both known inside and outside. For the last three categories also flag any unassigned B. Record the number of distinct B accounts per event, sharers, original items and quoters represented. Repeat for known between-group focal reposts as a separate comparison. If a denominator is zero, report the count and leave the rate undefined.

For a comparison between groups, show the size of each group, the number of distinct potential outside quoters observed for its items, and the resolution rate for each link. Compare groups on overlapping dates, query strata and item popularity. A raw rate difference can arise because one group replies more often, more outsiders quote its originals, or its replies more often contain the collection keywords.

| Check | Procedure and decision |
| --- | --- |
| Common attention, timing and chance co-occurrence | Compare before and after windows on the same focal events with symmetric coverage, using only quotes already published at the start of the earlier window on both sides. Recompute the before rate on this restricted support. Exclude ties and any comparison that would read into the M3 holdout. Subsequent replies can be genuine responses to the repost. Similar rates weaken an interpretation specific to prior ordering. For a burst-preserving comparison, shuffle the responding accounts among observed reply slots to quotes of the **same P**, within local hour and disjoint reply query context. Keep the slots, quote targets and all focal reposts fixed. Reapply actor and time restrictions for each draw. Report movable slots, distinct draws obtained and Monte Carlo uncertainty. This tests alignment beyond local activity under the stated exchangeability assumption. Sparse strata can make the comparison uninformative. |
| Attribution and missingness | Independently re-extract a stratified sample of positive joins and near misses on the host, blind to groups. Verify exact IDs, action direction, clocks, roles and duplicates against the archived records. Estimate agreement and record failure reasons for the observable join. Audit grouped, unassigned and rarely shared items, and the difference between candidate-query and wider lookup coverage. Selective capture of vocal repliers or candidate-naming quotes can change comparisons in either direction. |
| Concentration and uncertainty | Report by group, day, query, prior sharer activity and item popularity. Compare event weights with equal-sharer and equal-item estimates. Repeat using the first observed repost per sharer and item so one reply cannot explain many detected events. Remove leading items, quoters and the debate interval, recording the exclusion rule before inspecting contrasts and publishing the retained denominators. For the enumerated slice, X/D is a descriptive fraction. For stability, resample whole sharers and, separately, whole originals, and report leave-one-day-out results. Few contributing units or divergent checks warrant a narrower description. For claims about another period, replicate with frozen rules. |
| Political meaning | Preserve separate speakers for P, Q and H. Independently code a sample in Brazilian Portuguese for candidate, stance, criticism, irony, quotation, news framing and insufficient context if making a political claim. Examine whether a structural crossing is also a political disagreement. A contact observed only among people who eventually reposted supplies no denominator for choices among comparable aligned and opposing items. [Discourse protocol](reflexive-report-2.md) · [Echo-chamber criteria](reflexive-report-8.md#research-questions-and-evidence-needed-to-answer-them) |

A substantial X/D recurring across sharers and originals would establish that internal amplification regularly coexists with documented outward conversation about the same items in this sample. One verified case suffices to disprove an assertion that *every* internal repost was preceded exclusively by within-group interaction about that item. A result concentrated in a few viral posts or news accounts would characterize those cases. A small or zero X/D describes the frequency of the specified trace in the searched archive. Its implications for other contact depend on coverage. The fraction of reposts actually mediated by an outsider is unidentified at every value of X/D.

**A further political question.** If the joins and stance coding prove reliable, a separate design could start from *all* qualifying replies to quotes, including those followed by no recorded native repost. Index each sharer–item pair at its first observed eligible reply, exclude prior observed reposts of that item, require comparable prior and follow-up coverage, and count subsequent observed native reposts within a fixed horizon. Compare independently coded aligned and opposing originals within comparable accounts, days and topics, and code the quoter’s framing separately. This changes the denominator to items with a documented interaction. It could measure selective amplification among those interactions. Passive exposures, earlier unobserved contacts and the user's entire choice set would still be unknown. [Selective-uptake question](reflexive-report-8.md#research-questions-and-evidence-needed-to-answer-them)

## Testing a heuristic if stronger evidence becomes available

The first optional benchmark asks whether a method can retrieve **an account C demonstrably interacted with**. On held-out, independently reconciled cases with a prior same-item reply, hide all replies that supply the contact labels, their targets and derived features. Generate a common candidate set from authors who posted, quoted or reposted that exact item before R, retaining A and a way to abstain. Include quote authors so the documented B can enter on the same terms as other candidates. Use only actions available before R. For general tie strength, count earlier C-to-candidate replies or quotes on *other* items, excluding the focal item throughout. Freeze rules on development data and withhold originals across development and evaluation. Report missing candidates and tied times explicitly.

Compare uniform selection, the most recent eligible candidate, and recency constrained by earlier interactions. A follower-based variant requires a documented, time-appropriate account-pair snapshot with measured coverage. Compare candidate-set recall against all labeled cases, top-one and top-k retrieval of any documented B, coverage at abstention, and performance by candidate-set size and burst intensity. Report both unconditional performance and performance conditional on a documented B entering the set. Preserve all valid B labels per event, with a prespecified tie rule. Other candidates can have been real contacts. These scores validate retrieval of the observable proxy in a reply-selected sample. A claim of transmission-parent accuracy would need independent, event-linked referral or reshare-origin records and a definition for multiple exposures. Until such evidence exists, candidate weights can illustrate sensitivity to assumptions. They have no role as empirical parent probabilities or inputs to M3.

## Reproduction and handoff

The [reference SQL](../../scripts/repost_prior_contact.sql) returns one row per focal repost including zero-contact cases. It takes five pre-reconciled relations. `focal_reposts` holds repost ID, sharer, exact item, author, item and repost seconds, and the number of preceding seconds covered by the reply search frame. `quote_posts` holds quote ID, quoter, item ID and quote second. `direct_replies` holds reply ID, respondent, immediate target ID and reply second. Each has one row per action ID. `frozen_groups` maps each assigned account to one group. `contact_window` contains one positive duration in seconds. The query retains incomplete focal records for the coverage ledger. The extractor supplies the independent loss counts and excludes ambiguous evidence before creating these inputs.

Run `python3 scripts/check_repost_prior_contact.py` from the repository root for a dataset-free check. Ten fictional reposts cover mixed and unassigned contacts, repeated replies to one quoter, a different item, a repost of the quote, self interactions, missing clocks, clipped history and unresolved endpoints. The check exercises strict ordering, the inclusive upper lag bound and invariance of match counts when group labels are removed. In the fixture, one of four eligible internal reposts has outside contact. Restricting to events with classifiable contact would yield one of one. This difference illustrates why the corpus report must publish both denominators. Empirical accuracy, coverage and host performance require the archived data.

After the M3 baseline, the first host deliverable alongside the distinct-item extension is a discovery-only flow table of the joins, exclusions, denominators and blind verification results. Reuse the M2 reconciliation rules and frozen membership. The M2 aggregate and M3 repost ledger alone lack the prior typed reply and quote records. Build that time-stamped projection from the archived rows, including the pre-period buffer. Join replies to exact quotes before matching on sharer and item, and benchmark a bounded partition before scaling. Record snapshot, timezone, windows, query frame, code revision, input hashes, exclusion counts and seeds for resampling. Keep IDs and matched sequences on the host. Archive reviewed aggregates and the decision to replicate, narrow to a documented subset, or close with a feasibility finding. Freeze any confirmatory period, precision target and comparisons before opening its results.
