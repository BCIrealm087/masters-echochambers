# When does a hashtag graph recover candidate-relative stance?

**Incremental reflexive review · Report 4 · 2026-11-25**

Can a hashtag graph tell us how campaign language was used in Brazil’s 2022 runoff? Give stance classification a bounded methodological role. Test how reliably a label travels from a few manually coded hashtags to other tags, and then from a tag’s conventional meaning to its use in a post. Either step can fail independently. For the wider thesis, an independent stance measure could help interpret the political composition of account communities. The [held-out repost pilot](m1-advisor-synthesis.md#proposed-first-pilot) comes first and has its own behavioral outcome.

This unit consolidates the original review question and the supplied 11-page Deep Research report *Semi-Supervised Stance Classification and Graph Label Propagation for the Masters Degree – Echochambers Project*. Pages 1–4 cover the manuscript and constructs, pp. 4–6 rank methods, and pp. 6–9 propose validation and decisions. The review session could read the manuscript but could not access the project through public GitHub. The [pinned prior-code inventory](m1-ited-br-feasibility.md#what-can-be-recovered-from-the-earlier-study) resolves some of those unknowns. The protocol below records design decisions. Independent annotations, model comparisons and performance estimates are future work. [Q2 evidence addendum](m1-q2-evidence.md#reflexive-report-4-evidence-addendum)

## What can be reconstructed?

Revision 1 describes separate candidate queries from October 3 inclusive to October 31 exclusive. Three political scientists agreed on 409 Lula and 404 Bolsonaro hashtag seeds. Weighted co-occurrence and Gaussian Fields and Harmonic Functions (GFHF) yielded labels for candidate-specific graphs with 6,252 and 3,728 vertices respectively. Section 3.2 describes labeling the 500 most frequent tags per candidate, while §3.4 describes random selection. Both the choice of prominent tags and the retention of unanimous cases could favor easy campaign slogans. The manuscript supplies no untouched reference sample that measures propagation accuracy across the frequency distribution. [Prior study and revision record](../prior-study.md)

The archived code deduplicates post IDs, normalizes tags, retains the largest connected component and loads completed workbooks into a Laplace solver. It cannot tell us how the annotators’ items were drawn. Exact seeds and per-class counts, hydrated inputs and exclusions, the executed revision, package version and settings remain unresolved. A new annotation pool permits a **reconstruction** and a common benchmark. Report the historical largest-component filter when matching the published specification, then count all eligible components and excluded tags when measuring benchmark coverage. Public files sampled in M1 lacked hashtags and text. Audit a permitted extract separately for tags, authored text, original language, dates, author IDs and quote/reply context, with missingness by candidate and time. A tag-only extract can support a type-level graph test once labels exist. Context and account claims require additional evidence. [Feasibility audit](m1-ited-br-feasibility.md)

## What receives a label?

| Unit and target | Evidence and judgment | Meaning for the thesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normalized hashtag type × candidate | Judge conventional campaign meaning in the period. Record favorable, opposed, neutral/descriptive, ambiguous/mixed or unknown. | This is the direct target of propagation. The same string can have different meanings relative to Lula and Bolsonaro. |
| A particular use × candidate | Inspect the authored Brazilian Portuguese passage, hashtag bundle and relevant parent/quoted text. Code expressed stance, mixed or indeterminate use, and flag irony, quotation or appropriation separately. | Estimate when a type label transfers to use. A hostile quotation of a supportive slogan can preserve the slogan’s conventional meaning while reversing the speaker’s stance. Attribute reposted words to their source. |
| Account × candidate × specified interval | For a separately sampled subset, independently code multiple authored posts and record changing, mixed or insufficient evidence. | Estimates expressed orientation over that interval. A hashtag count or a repost alone cannot establish an account holder’s beliefs. |

Retain raw judgments and distinguish disagreement, unavailable context and model abstention. Freeze a crosswalk to the historical favorable/opposed/neutral-or-undefined task for comparable scores, with separate counts for each ambiguous or excluded category. Use Report 2’s speaker-attribution rules for contextual coding. [Q3 measurement design](reflexive-report-2.md)

## Which comparisons are worth running?

Freeze the post IDs, normalization, candidate queries, dates, repost policy, distinct tag incidence per post, nodes, seeds and evaluation splits. A graph vertex is a hashtag within a candidate query. An undirected edge records two distinct tags used in the same post. Check on development labels whether linked endpoints share stance more often than expected under label permutations within candidate and frequency bands. Report the fraction of graph edges and weight with labels at both ends. A test confined to labeled hubs tells us little about the tail.

| Priority and cost | Experiment | Question it can resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · low solver cost | Cross raw and M1 fractional edges with GFHF (fixed seeds), LGC (soft seed fidelity) and Poisson learning (seed sources and sinks). Include majority-class, one-hop weighted vote and a nearest-seed control. | Do neighbors predict stance, does propagation beyond one hop help, and does Poisson help in sparsely seeded regions? Count seedless components as uncovered. |
| 2 · low to moderate | Fit TF-IDF with a linear classifier and a Brazilian Portuguese encoder such as BERTabaporu, with BERTimbau as a general-language comparator if feasible. Compare on identical targets, cases and split rules. | What does graph structure add beyond the words? Compare tag strings on the type task and full posts on the use task. Score the transferred type prediction on those same uses. |
| 3 · moderate to high | A bipartite account–hashtag graph preserves observed account use. Try simple propagation first. | Does retaining who used a tag resolve errors lost in projection? Record any overlap with evidence used to discover account groups. |
| 4–5 · moderate to high | Consider one graph-plus-text model if both channels show complementary errors. Consider a heterophily-aware method if unlike stances frequently co-occur. Signed edges require separately observed or estimated signs and a check on their errors. | Does the extra input improve a matched test at useful coverage? Co-occurrence alone supplies no edge sign. |
| 6–7 · high | Check week-by-week drift before a temporal model. Defer large end-to-end GNN or LLM models until label volume and residual errors justify the cost. | Would model complexity explain a reproducible failure of the simpler methods? |

For a post with k distinct retained tags (k ≥ 2), **raw** adds 1 per pair and **M1 fractional** adds 1/(k−1) per pair, totaling k/2 per post. The review also suggests fixing each post’s *total* weight. Register **equal-post weighting** as a separate sensitivity, adding 2/[k(k−1)] per pair. With 20 tags the totals are 190, 10 and 1 respectively. Run the six primary graph configurations before comparing the equal-post variant under the same splits. None of these rules caps the influence of many reposts of one original. Record source or author concentration separately. [Graph specification](m1-graph-methods.md)

## How would the benchmark be judged?

1. **Freeze an independent reference.** Proposed planning targets are 300–500 hashtag types and 900–1,500 contextual uses across the two candidates. About 600 uses is a smaller design with wider subgroup intervals. Stratify by candidate, log frequency, component and graph position, cap the most frequent stratum and document inclusion probabilities. Sample uses within types and across weeks. Report type-weighted and use-weighted results with their denominators. Plan the account subset separately. Three fluent Brazilian Portuguese annotators pilot the codebook, then code independently. Freeze the original votes before adjudication and report chance-corrected agreement by target and unit, along with distributions and ambiguity. These targets are planning estimates. The reference labels still have to be collected.
2. **Keep the final test untouched.** Use separate seed/training, development and final reference pools. Group spelling and near-duplicate slogan families across partitions, group copies of the same original text, and split by account for the account task. Keep cross-candidate labels from leaking through equivalent items. For transductive graph scoring, held-out nodes and their unlabeled edges may appear in the frozen graph. Keep all test judgments out of tuning, graph design, class priors, calibration, stopping and abstention choices. Use a separately frozen later slice for a temporal transfer claim.
3. **Test where supervision lands.** Compare frequent-first, uniform, frequency-stratified, degree-stratified and graph-coverage seed selection at matched budgets. Repeat across recorded draws and thinner seed budgets, including 10–20% removal. When using human agreement or edge compatibility to revise the design, use pilot or development judgments only. Record class counts, seeded components, seed distances and balance of graph regions. Report separately what can be estimated if only frequent historical labels can be recovered.
4. **Compare errors at useful coverage.** Report macro-F1, balanced accuracy, per-class precision/recall/F1 and confusion matrices. Give naturally weighted results alongside class-balanced summaries, paired differences and uncertainty accounting for repeated uses of a type or original. Break down by candidate, week, frequency, component size and distance to a seed. Count missing nodes, isolates, seedless components and abstentions in the eligible denominator. Show performance on all eligible cases and on commonly covered cases. Tune calibration on development data, inspect reliability, and plot error or macro-F1 against coverage. Freeze class-specific precision goals and a minimum useful coverage before opening the test. Report which classes or frequency bands disappear first.
5. **Preserve the run.** Keep a manifest with snapshot/hash, time zone and window, input rights, preprocessing and split IDs, vocabulary, weighted edge list, component statistics, seed provenance, class mapping, library versions, random seeds, solver settings, pre-decision score vectors and thresholds. Record annotation effort, wall time, peak memory and convergence with evaluation outputs. Keep identifiable data and workbooks in approved local storage. Archive shareable specifications and aggregates under the [project instructions](../../AGENTS.md).

## What would change the thesis conclusion?

| Observation on independent evidence | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Annotators disagree about conventional meaning, or independently coded contextual uses frequently reverse or obscure it. | Limit type claims to stable strata. Move the substantive stance question to contextual Portuguese text where available. |
| Adjacent tags have little same-stance enrichment after frequency control. | Retain GFHF and LGC as diagnostic baselines. Investigate text or alternate relations before using a graph score downstream. |
| Rankings reverse with modest seed or weighting changes, or gains concentrate on common slogans. | Report dependence on supervision or projection. Name the supported range and retain the simpler baseline elsewhere. |
| At matched coverage, graph methods offer no practically useful gain over cheap controls, or the precision target leaves too few accounts or classes represented. | Publish the negative comparison and its uncertainty. Omit unsupported account or group estimates. |
| Stance and account-community discovery reuse the same hashtag or account–hashtag relations. | Treat agreement as a description. To test political composition, freeze communities and evaluate independently sampled authored text or separately coded account histories, recording evidence provenance and coverage by group. |

The next attainable Q2 milestone is an inventory of permitted hashtag and contextual fields followed by a frozen reference protocol. Once the necessary evidence exists, start with the controlled graph and text baselines. A stable improvement or a documented limit on how far hashtag stance travels can each support a standalone methods contribution. A claim about selective circulation still requires the separate [repost evidence](reflexive-report-3.md).
