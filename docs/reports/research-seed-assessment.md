# What can a repost community tell us about a political divide?

**Research design assessment after M1 and reflexive reports 1–8 · 2026-11-26**

The project has a defensible Computer Science question and a reason political researchers would want its answer. Can account groups learned during Brazil’s 2022 runoff predict whom their members amplify later? If they can, do the group boundaries also tell us which political messages circulate, who produces them, and which messages cross those boundaries? The first question tests the graph. The second tests the value of interpreting it politically. The repository is ready for a feasibility pilot. Both questions are empirically open. This assessment covers the [repository through Report 8](https://github.com/BCIrealm087/masters-echochambers/tree/1db233fd42372935486656cfc9b87ae6ae0e0573) and Revision 1 of the researcher’s prior manuscript.

## Where the proposal is vulnerable

**The denominator is the biggest empirical risk.** The collection paper reports 282,135,572 posts across contexts, including duplicates and some older referenced items. The public-file inspection verified seven Parquet schemas for 16 October. Sampled post files contain action, author and reference identifiers, but lack explicit interaction type, creation time and text. The repository has no executable thesis pilot or counts of distinct, verified, dated reposts linked to historical authors. A further loss happened during collection itself. Ordinary candidate searches excluded reposts and the separate retrieval was incomplete across the corpus. Better historical lookup can fix some broken links. It cannot recover actions the collectors never observed. The Interfaces collaboration is valuable here because it gives the project a route to investigate how records were produced and whether a minimal historical extract exists. Access to that extract remains unverified. [Schema inspection](m1-ited-br-feasibility.md#what-was-verified) · [Feasibility review](reflexive-report-7.md) · [Collection paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0316626)

**The broad idea already has close precedents.** Interian and Rodrigues studied communities and daily modularity in a repost network from the same election. They found a substantial press community alongside left- and right-leaning groups in their sample. Malagoli and colleagues connected users who shared the same items and examined topics, sentiment and language on the two election days. Building a graph, finding clusters and describing their content would leave the added contribution unclear. A stronger contribution would be a reproducible test of what a group predicts beyond past ties and item popularity, how much incomplete observation can change that answer, and whether the structural result agrees with independently assessed content. Novelty at that level is a proposition to demonstrate through comparisons. [Interian and Rodrigues](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-072X/acf6a4) · [Malagoli et al.](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/webmedia/article/download/30323/30129/)

**The main risk to the original vision is losing the political question in the methods.** M1 ranks five avenues, and the eight reviews specify several viable follow-ups. They are design work. Implementing every clustering comparison, stance model, rhetoric label and LLM evaluation could consume a master’s without answering what an apparent political divide consists of. I would protect one content question from the outset and let data access determine when it can be answered. Keep model-generated explanations optional and use independent Portuguese-speaking reviewers where attribution or stance actually needs judgment. [Ranked agenda](m1-advisor-synthesis.md#ranked-shortlist) · [Explanation benchmark](reflexive-report-1.md)

## One political comparison worth protecting

Revision 1 interpreted the hashtag score distributions as suggesting particularly negative campaigning against Lula (§8, p. 17). A direct follow-up would ask whether criticism of each candidate appears across many group members’ own writing or gains prominence through repeated sharing of a few originals. Estimate candidate-directed criticism separately from personal attacks. Compare the average participating author, distinct amplified originals, and amplification weighted by repost count on comparable days and topics. Code both candidates independently and include mixed and journalistic cases. If a contrast appears only when reposts are counted repeatedly, a claim about the visibility of particular messages is warranted. If it also appears across many authors’ own writing, that supports a broader linguistic characterization of the group. Either outcome would revisit the earlier interpretation on new evidence. [Manuscript record](../prior-study.md) · [Authorship and circulation measures](reflexive-report-2.md#4-compare-groups-using-four-explicit-denominators)

## What would justify the next investment?

1. **Establish the usable slice.** For the proposed 16–21 October window, the first output should be a reproducible command, input manifest and aggregate table by day and query. Count candidate rows, unique verified reposts, actions with reliable São Paulo dates, and referenced posts linked to a unique historical author. Keep unknowns visible and measure uneven losses. Inventory a small permitted text sample separately if one is available. This tells the advisor which thesis questions the collection can actually support. [Feasibility decision](reflexive-report-7.md#decision-sequence-and-alternatives)
2. **If the slice supports the comparison, test one structural claim.** Discover on 16–18 October, freeze the analysis, and test on 19–21 October. Compare the observed excess with the prespecified reference and the predictive gain over prior ties. Check how much one viral original, a dominant author, or plausible selective loss can change the conclusion. The discovery window includes the post-debate peak. A claim beyond those dates needs an untouched interval. A precise negligible effect would bound what membership adds. Wide uncertainty would leave the question open. [Pilot and decision rules](m1-advisor-synthesis.md#proposed-first-pilot) · [Collection paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0316626)
3. **Choose the extension supported by the result.** With credible groups and original-post IDs, compare which items circulate across boundaries. With adequate independently coded text, take up the candidate-specific comparison above, retaining groups with weak or mixed political evidence. If event dating or author recovery defeats the test, record which comparisons fail, how large the uncertainty is, and whether a minimal historical extract or a separately audited election network can resolve it. A quantified demonstration of when the inference fails could itself contribute to CS. A file-access failure alone would leave that contribution open. [Source alternatives](reflexive-report-7.md#decision-sequence-and-alternatives) · [Conceptual falsifiers](reflexive-report-8.md#what-would-change-the-conclusion)

The thesis can succeed if well-measured social boundaries predict very little about political content. That outcome would answer a question the original hashtag graph left open and tell political researchers how much interpretive weight to give the next community map.
