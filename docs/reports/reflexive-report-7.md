# Can ITED-Br support a historical repost graph?

**Incremental reflexive review · Report 7 · 2026-11-25**

The public ITED-Br release supports a measurement-feasibility pilot. A substantive test of internal repost amplification depends on identifying true reposts, recovering their historical endpoints, dating the actions and showing that plausible losses preserve the conclusion. A high target-match percentage addresses only one of those questions. An approved historical relational extract would shorten the reconstruction, although even that extract inherits the original collection gaps.

## What the evidence establishes

The supplied 17-page review, *ITED-Br Feasibility and Methodological Validity for Echo-Chamber Research*, examined the collection paper, public dataset and code documentation, missing-network methods and alternative datasets. The reviewer could neither inspect the thesis repository nor retrieve a public Parquet object or immutable Git revision. Its field audit (pp. 3–7) describes documented fields and tests to perform. The project’s earlier pinned inspection adds seven Parquet schemas for 16 October 2022 and directory listings for four contexts. Those observations establish the presence of some identifiers. Neither investigation measured event counts after deduplication, type-classification accuracy, join yield or a political-network effect. [Step 2 observations and pinned samples](m1-ited-br-feasibility.md#what-was-verified) · [Collection paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0316626) · [Collection code archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.13124582)

**Two losses arise at different times.** The Portuguese candidate searches used changing names, nicknames and number terms. Tebet was added retrospectively, Bolsonaro terms changed during the campaign, and special event queries broadened the frame. Ordinary candidate searches initially excluded reposts. October volume delayed their separate acquisition, and API limits and the end of academic access interrupted it. The collectors report no Ciro or Simone reposts after 27 October 2022 and no Lula, Bolsonaro or post-election reposts after 13 January 2023. These cutoffs establish differential collection over the corpus. Completeness on 16–21 October is still unknown. A second loss occurs when a *recorded* action lacks a recoverable target author. Perfect linkage could solve the latter while leaving the former intact. Estimates apply to observed activity under the named queries. Keep query membership distinct from political stance. [Collection paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0316626)

Compare against independently dated actions across contexts and boundaries. Retain original creation time, collection time and partition date as distinct fields. Older originals may occur in later partitions. Without reliable dates the chronological test stops. |
| Provenance, text and accounts | Retain the pinned release, query memberships, path, partition and type/date evidence for each action. The README’s 2023–24 dates and the paper’s 2022–23 publication period describe an unresolved dating discrepancy. Historical text is optional for the structural pilot. Text-dependent work needs its own coverage audit. Timestamp account snapshots and keep current profiles separate from historical attributes. |

Derive count-weighted edges **sharer → original author** from distinct eligible repost IDs. A wider target index supplies historical identities only. Keep the action windows fixed and exclude later activity or popularity from discovery. A typed, dated action with a known post and unknown author may still enter the [distinct-post analysis](reflexive-report-3.md). A known author outside the frozen partition counts as external for the [Report 5 test](reflexive-report-5.md). Neither case is interchangeable with a missing repost. The graph measures observed amplification. Endorsement, exposure, intermediary paths, trust and reinforcement each require other evidence.

## Measure the effect of missingness

The critical threats are incomplete acquisition, ambiguous action type and selective loss of target authors. Query boundaries and duplicate weights come next, followed by time errors, conflating local and present-day availability, and degree-dependent loss. Independent stance evidence and stable group assignments matter when interpreting any surviving pattern. Report the following denominators with counts and undefined values for empty or unidentified populations. [Kossinets, Effects of missing data in social networks](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socnet.2005.07.002)

| Coverage question | Denominator and comparison |
| --- | --- |
| How many known actions become edges? | All distinct independently typed reposts in the verified window. Tabulate missing sharers, missing references, ambiguous authors and successful joins. Report separately the assigned-sharer subset used for inference. Unknown type leaves eligibility unresolved. |
| Does repetition conceal missing sources or actors? | Compare event-weighted recovery with recovery across distinct referenced post IDs and the share of distinct sharers with resolvable actions. Estimate historical unique-author coverage only where a validation source identifies the denominator. |
| Where do joins fail? | Report by day, interval, candidate query and overlap, assigned sharing group, activity, target-ID reuse and edge weight where observable. Examine target in-degree among resolved authors. Estimate internal versus cross-group recovery only on independently recovered pairs and report validation-sample selection. |

For a fixed group of known sharers, let I and E count recorded actions with known internal and external destinations, and U other eligible recorded actions with unknown destination group. The internal share lies between I/(I+E+U) and (I+U)/(I+E+U). With 49 internal, 21 external and 30 unknown, it ranges from **49% to 79%**. The 30-point width concerns the raw share of these recorded actions. It excludes never-collected reposts and sampling uncertainty. **Retire the 70% pass rule.** Prespecify the effect size and tolerance before seeing outcomes. For excess above expected sharing, recompute both observed and reference shares on matching events for each completion. A raw-share interval alone cannot establish the sign or size of that excess.

Compare plausible corpus boundaries (common window and contexts, query union, inclusion of event-specific terms where available). Examine counts, spikes, cutoff discontinuities and deduplication effects. Reweight using variables available on both resolved and unresolved actions only under an explicit assumption of conditionally random loss. Then tilt unresolved ties toward internal or cross-group destinations, one camp, viral originals and hubs. Calibrate removals or rewirings to observed losses within strata. Report where the direction or practical interpretation changes, and stability of mixing, modularity, assortativity, components, reciprocity and hub ranks as relevant. If the discovery graph changes, refit each discovery partition and freeze it before its paired holdout test. Keep the original prespecified partition for the primary test. ## Decision sequence and alternatives

1. **Verify schema, type and time on heterogeneous partitions.** Reconcile row and event counts, exclusion reasons, provenance and date precision. Ambiguous type stops repost inference. Ambiguous dating permits a partition audit only.
2. **Join locally across the release.** Build the global historical-author lookup before considering API recovery. A bounded initial lookup can diagnose cost, but its failures remain provisional. Where permitted, validate stratified samples of resolved and unresolved records against an approved historical source or current lookup. Report present-day hydration yield separately.
3. **Decide against the intended estimate.** Run stratified bounds and the specified missingness tests. Proceed with an exploratory within-corpus result only if the directional and practical conclusions survive credible scenarios at the required precision. Record failure, narrow the population with coverage-only criteria, or redesign if type, targeting, differential collection or time remains decisive.
4. **Check transfer before scaling.** The anonymized [Mendeley election retweet network, v2](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/x7ypgrzr3m/2) is the first benchmark. Its generic election-keyword frame covers 17 September–30 October and the associated study orients edges from author to reposter. Align direction, dates, weight and estimand. Verify released granularity before choosing comparisons. An aggregate account graph supports graph-level comparisons only where post/day strata cannot be reconstructed. Investigate reversals in light of distinct frames and keep datasets separate. [Associated study](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-072X/acf6a4)
5. **Choose the minimum viable source.** Prefer an approved ITED-Br historical extract with event ID, sharing account, action time and type, referenced ID and historical author, query memberships and duplicate provenance. It can omit text and profiles. The [Pacheco masked relational archive](https://zenodo.org/records/10669936) offers a timestamped alternative subject to a collection audit. The [trending-topic election archive](https://zenodo.org/records/14834749) better fits event-specific content questions. Current rehydration describes survivors and belongs in a validation sample. Each route has its own observation frame.

Archive hashes, schemas, manifest, source and code revisions, timezone, classification and deduplication rules, graph direction and weights, stratified denominators, software, commands, perturbation settings and negative results. Keep identifiable ledgers local and publish disclosure-reviewed aggregates. Confirm permitted access, retention, deletion/protection handling and publication terms for any historical extract or rehydration and document the applicable institutional review. The next empirical deliverable is an audited ledger and feasibility decision. The substantive holdout and independent benchmark remain pending.
