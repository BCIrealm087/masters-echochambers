# Research directions and first pilot for the ITED-Br thesis

**M1 · Advisor synthesis · 2026-11-25**

Can a group of accounts discovered in one part of Brazil’s 2022 presidential election show selective sharing in a later period? **Start with a feasibility audit of observed reposts.** If type, event dates, historical endpoints and robustness to selective loss support the comparison, detect groups in one window, freeze their membership, and test whether those groups repost internally more often than expected given sharer activity and original-post popularity within each day and collection context. Make target-author coverage and sensitivity to missing links part of the result. This is a tractable extension of the earlier hashtag study and a basis for later claims about political stance, discourse and information flow. [Prior study](../prior-study.md) · [Review protocol](m1-review-protocol.md)

## What the milestone established

The earlier study linked hashtags that appeared together and used Gaussian Fields and Harmonic Functions (GFHF) to infer stance relative to each candidate. That work provides a baseline for campaign vocabulary. ITED-Br’s public release permits a different unit of analysis. The sampled post files preserved post, author and referenced-post IDs, and October folders exist for the four candidate-query and repost contexts used in the prior study. The remaining measurement questions are which rows are true repost events, when those events occurred, how often their historical authors resolve, and how differential acquisition and linkage affect the estimate. The same samples did not contain text, hashtags, URLs or an explicit creation timestamp. The collection documentation describes those fields for the original corpus. Access to a suitable extract remains unverified. [ITED-Br feasibility report](m1-ited-br-feasibility.md) · [ITED-Br collection paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0316626)

Interian and Rodrigues already used an account repost network to study Brazil’s 2022 election, including group-level modularity and network reach. The recommended contribution is to test whether later observed behavior supports a group interpretation and how much the answer depends on query selection, missing endpoints and a few very active sources. The primary comparison fixes each sharer’s activity and each original’s repost count within day and collection context. A separate predictive comparison measures what frozen membership adds beyond historical sharer–author ties. It leaves the original collection process and what users actually saw outside the inference. [Interian and Rodrigues, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-072X/acf6a4) · [Hobson et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12775) · [Group detection and circulation review](m1-echo-chamber-detection.md)

## Proposed first pilot

**Immediate question.** Can the public files support the intended historical comparison at the required precision? The [Report 7 audit](reflexive-report-7.md) is the first deliverable.

**Conditional research question.** How much excess internal reposting survives controls for activity, collection context, original-post popularity and historical relationships? Which author groups receive that amplification? Do the same communities share distinct or overlapping original-post repertoires? The unit is one observed repost action. Graph vertices are accounts and each directed edge runs from the sharing account to the referenced author, with weight equal to the number of distinct resolved actions. In circulation tables, explicitly label the referenced author as the origin and the sharing account as the destination. The result concerns observed reposts in these collection contexts.

**Initial slice.** Pin the public release to the commit inspected in Step 2. Use 16–18 October 2022 as the provisional discovery interval and 19–21 October as the holdout, using the São Paulo calendar after verifying event-time conventions. These six days sit within the previous study’s October window, include the date already checked for file schema, and avoid the October 4 query change and October 30 election-day spike. The 17 October post-debate peak could produce a short-lived discovery partition. Select on date, schema and coverage before calculating the holdout effect. Use one neighboring partition on either side only if needed for calendar boundaries. If event dating or stable chronological assignment cannot be verified, report coverage by partition and defer the temporal validation. **First structural extension.** Given typed post references and frozen sharer groups, test whether groups share more or fewer distinct originals than expected from account activity and item popularity. Later, when author joins allow, discover producer clusters from audience overlap and compare them with the account communities. For discovery, a directed degree-corrected block model tests whether shared source roles explain the account partition. A native user–post comparison starts on common events before expanding to post references with unknown authors. ### Decision criteria

The figures below are proposed pilot planning defaults. They should be frozen after the schema and coverage check and before viewing measured excesses. They define where to spend the next round of work. Treat any positive result as exploratory and specific to the observed sample.

| Result | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| **Action type or event period cannot be established.** | Complete the coverage report. Restrict the empirical analysis to a typed and dated subset, or defer the repost-based holdout test until the required provenance can be recovered. |
| **Selective acquisition or unresolved targets could change the conclusion.** Record coverage by event, distinct post and sharer, time, provenance, activity and validation-known group pair. | Use the global lookup, stratum-specific bounds and plausible selective-loss scenarios against the prespecified effect tolerance. If the decision changes, report feasibility or a coverage-defined narrower population. Seek a minimal approved historical extract if the public route cannot recover the required relations. Compare common measures on the independent election network before scaling. [Report 7 decisions](reflexive-report-7.md#decision-sequence-and-alternatives) |
| **Global structural excess and secondary group evidence.** Prespecify the one-sided global test at 0.05. Use 100 resolved events from 20 assigned sharers as a local reporting screen, Holm adjustment for local inference and +5 percentage points as a provisional practical screen. | Report effect sizes, sensitivity to alternative cutoffs and network-simulation power. Replicate the frozen design on an untouched interval. An effect confined to a hub, repeated dyad or original narrows the interpretation. Independent stance or content is needed for political alignment. [Statistical decision rules](reflexive-report-5.md#decision-rules-power-and-uncertainty) |
| **Adequate data, with weak or unstable enrichment.** Evidence excludes a practically meaningful effect, or the result changes materially with graph weights, query or concentration checks. | Keep the negative or conditional finding. Label estimates with insufficient precision inconclusive. Review mismatches in discovery and holdout, document the method sensitivity, and justify any second design before evaluating it on fresh held-out data. |
| **Too little or too selectively observed evidence after narrowing.** | Stop expansion of this pilot and document the specific data dependency. A smaller reproducible figure for linkage coverage is still a useful result. |

## Decisions for the next milestone

The proposed pilot can use the public files and scripted checks. The important decision for the next milestone is the scope of the first result. A structural study can test whether groups repeatedly share internally and report the limits of held-out evidence. A claim about political selectivity additionally needs independently labeled stance or discourse, enough textual coverage, and a comparator aligned with that claim. If the latter becomes essential, ask the research group for the minimum approved extract or location of the original label workbooks. Their involvement becomes necessary when those inputs are unavailable through the public route or a domain judgment cannot be established by automated checks.
