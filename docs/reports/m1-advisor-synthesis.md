# Research directions and first pilot for the ITED-Br thesis

**M1 · Advisor synthesis · 2026-11-25**

Can a group of accounts discovered in one part of Brazil’s 2022 presidential election show selective sharing in a later period? **Start with a public-data pilot on observed reposts.** Detect groups in one window, freeze their membership, and test whether those groups repost internally more often than expected given account activity and popularity. Make target-author coverage and sensitivity to missing links part of the result. This is a tractable extension of the earlier hashtag study and a basis for later claims about political stance, discourse and information flow. [Prior study](../prior-study.md) · [Review protocol](m1-review-protocol.md)

## What the milestone established

The earlier study linked hashtags that appeared together and used Gaussian Fields and Harmonic Functions (GFHF) to infer stance relative to each candidate. That work provides a baseline for campaign vocabulary. ITED-Br’s public release permits a different unit of analysis. The sampled post files preserved post, author and referenced-post IDs, and October folders exist for the four candidate-query and repost contexts used in the prior study. The remaining technical issue is how often a repost’s referenced ID can be joined to its author and how evenly those joins succeed across queries and intervals. The same samples did not contain text, hashtags, URLs or an explicit creation timestamp. The collection documentation describes those fields for the original corpus. Access to a suitable extract remains unverified. [ITED-Br feasibility report](m1-ited-br-feasibility.md) · [ITED-Br collection paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0316626)

Interian and Rodrigues already used an account repost network to study Brazil’s 2022 election, including group-level modularity and network reach. The recommended contribution is to test whether independently held-out behavior supports a group interpretation and how much the answer depends on query selection, missing endpoints and a few very active sources. The degree-preserving reference model compares the observed allocation of reposts with what would be expected under the same activity totals. It addresses one source of confounding in the observed data. It leaves the original collection process and what users actually saw outside the inference. [Interian and Rodrigues, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-072X/acf6a4) · [Hobson et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12775) · [Group detection and circulation review](m1-echo-chamber-detection.md)

## Proposed first pilot

**Question.** Can we recover enough typed repost-to-author links from the public release to detect account groups and estimate excess internal reposting on held-out events? The unit is one observed repost action. Graph vertices are accounts and each directed edge runs from the sharing account to the referenced author, with weight equal to the number of distinct resolved actions. In circulation tables, explicitly label the referenced author as the origin and the sharing account as the destination. The result concerns observed reposts in these collection contexts.

**Initial slice.** Pin the public release to the commit inspected in Step 2. Use 16–18 October 2022 as the provisional discovery interval and 19–21 October as the holdout, using the São Paulo calendar after verifying event-time conventions. These six days sit within the previous study’s October window, include the date already checked for file schema, and avoid the October 4 query change and October 30 election-day spike. Select on date, schema and coverage before calculating the holdout effect. Use one neighboring partition on either side only if needed for calendar boundaries. If event dating or stable chronological assignment cannot be verified, report coverage by partition and defer the temporal validation. [Pinned public release](https://github.com/Interfaces-UFSCAR/ITED-Br/tree/deae9a6ca931882ab736c2119e600af28f5d89cb) · [Step 2 coverage](m1-ited-br-feasibility.md#coverage-and-the-comparison-window) · [Interian and Rodrigues](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-072X/acf6a4)

| Operation | Comparison and expected output |

| **Audit the event table and join targets.** Read the two public repost-query contexts for actions. Build the target lookup from their post records and the ordinary Lula and Bolsonaro query post records for the same bounded slice. Verify from collection provenance and available checks what qualifies as a repost in the relevant partitions. | Record source files and hashes, observed schemas, ID uniqueness, data types, split assignment and the justification for action type. | **Fit a small structural baseline.** Use directed Louvain on the resolved discovery events, with fixed settings and recorded random seeds. Freeze the resulting partition before viewing test results. | Record graph size, components, membership stability and unassigned counts. Rerun directed Louvain on distinct account pairs as the first sensitivity check, align groups where possible, and report splits and merges. Retain partition settings and set any group reporting thresholds before opening the holdout. |
| **Evaluate the frozen groups.** Use the same held-out event table for each partition. Compare each group’s observed internal repost share with the expected share and the central 95% simulated range under a directed reference preserving repost counts made and received. | Report observed and expected proportions, their difference in percentage points, sample counts and unresolved shares. Preserve day or disjoint query-membership strata where the reference model has enough freedom. Declare how self-links and repeated edges are handled. Recheck query-specific results where feasible, and repeat the main comparison after excluding the most-amplified source in the relevant sample, recalculating the reference on that reduced set. Unassigned accounts remain visible in the coverage report. |
| **Summarize the observed circulation and report the decision.** Generate one aggregate origin-by-sharing-group table, with distinct original posts and sharing events, and a separate unknown-endpoint category. | Publish the manifest, commands, versions, timings, aggregate coverage and group results. Give each reported group a disposition and record whether the next step is further structural validation, a narrower coverage study or a text-based extension. |

For each group, also report the lower and upper within-group shares if all observed unresolved target assignments from its known members were external or internal. Compare rates and reference distributions on matching sets of events. The bounds for all observed events cannot be read as a test against the reference fitted only on resolved events. If missing links are substantial, repeat the paired analysis under a bounded change to the lookup and report the remaining uncertainty. ### Decision criteria

The figures below are proposed pilot planning defaults. They should be frozen after the schema and coverage check and before viewing measured excesses. They define where to spend the next round of work. Treat any positive result as exploratory and specific to the observed sample.

| Result | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| **Action type or event period cannot be established.** | Complete the coverage report. Restrict the empirical analysis to a typed and dated subset, or defer the repost-based holdout test until the required provenance can be recovered. |
| **Uneven or inadequate target-author coverage.** Start with a 70% resolved-target screening threshold in each candidate repost context and interval. Inspect concentration of unresolved events even above that threshold. | Narrow to a documented slice that passes or perform one extension of the source lookup, limited to three neighboring days before and three after the main slice. Choose this based on coverage before examining group outcomes. If uncertainty remains consequential, retain a feasibility result and avoid a pooled behavioral conclusion. |
| **Promising structural result.** A reportable group has at least 100 resolved held-out reposts from 20 assigned sharers, exceeds the reference expectation by at least five percentage points, and exceeds the upper end of the central 95% simulated reference range in the primary specification. The interpretation survives the feasible concentration and coverage checks. | Carry the structural method into an independent slice or a larger prespecified pilot. Describe excess internal reposting in the observed sample. Consider independent stance or text evidence before moving to a political echo-chamber claim. |
| **Adequate data, with weak or unstable enrichment.** The group does not clear the comparison or the result changes materially with graph weights, query or concentration checks. | Keep the negative or conditional finding. Review mismatches in discovery and holdout, document the method sensitivity, and justify any second design before evaluating it on fresh held-out data. |
| **Too little or too selectively observed evidence after narrowing.** | Stop expansion of this pilot and document the specific data dependency. A smaller reproducible figure for linkage coverage is still a useful result. |

## Decisions for the next milestone

The proposed pilot can use the public files and scripted checks. The important decision for the next milestone is the scope of the first result. A structural study can test whether groups repeatedly share internally and report the limits of held-out evidence. A claim about political selectivity additionally needs independently labeled stance or discourse, enough textual coverage, and a comparator aligned with that claim. If the latter becomes essential, ask the research group for the minimum approved extract or location of the original label workbooks. Their involvement becomes necessary when those inputs are unavailable through the public route or a domain judgment cannot be established by automated checks.
