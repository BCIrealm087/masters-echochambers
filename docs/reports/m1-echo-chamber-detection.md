# Detecting candidate echo chambers in ITED-Br

**M1 · Step 3 · 2026-11-24 · Revised by [Reflexive report 3](reflexive-report-3.md) on 2026-11-25**

What would show that an account community in Brazil’s 2022 presidential election had a pattern of selective sharing? The clearest test available to this project is whether communities detected in one period show excess internal reposting in a separate period after accounting for account activity and popularity. Independent content or stance evidence can address whether that sharing is politically selective. Missing references and the original collection queries limit both claims. The public ITED-Br schemas support attempting the joins needed for the structural test. [Step 2 feasibility report](m1-ited-br-feasibility.md) · [Search log and evidence notes](m1-q1-evidence.md)

## What the prior work establishes

Interian and Rodrigues already built a directed repost graph for this election from Portuguese election terms, using a collection of about 15.2 million posts from 17 September to 30 October 2022. They detected groups using interactions through 29 October and examined group contributions to modularity and network reach. Their large groups included a news-media group alongside two political groups. Dinkelberg, Santana and Bazzan also published a descriptive account of candidate-centered retweet networks for the same election. Their study is recorded as a lead in the evidence notes while its full methods remain unchecked. The thesis can contribute a test of group-level validity and robustness to query selection and missing endpoints. Their keyword sample gives a useful methodological comparison with the candidate-centered ITED-Br queries, and their decision to handle election day separately gives us an explicit sensitivity check. We have not established how much the two collections overlap. [Interian and Rodrigues, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-072X/acf6a4)

For an earlier Brazilian context, Cota et al. combined hashtag-based stance, a communication network and simulated spreading to study the Dilma Rousseff impeachment. Their simulations estimate what could spread on the modeled graph, with results conditional on the model and its selected strongly connected component. The ITED-Br thesis can start with actually observed repost events and add simulation if a later question needs it. For Brazil’s 2022 election, Ferreira et al. also show how group-specific discourse can be studied with text. Their coding of the 50 most reposted messages from each selected cluster describes prominent messages about the electoral system. A group-wide comparison in this thesis would also need to account for ordinary and low-activity contributions. [Cota et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-019-0213-9) · [Ferreira et al.](https://doi.org/10.26694/2317-3254.rcp.v13i2.6933)

## Definitions and evidence

| Interpretation | What would support it | What would weaken it or limit the claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Structural concentration of observed sharing.** Accounts share from their own detected group more often than expected. | A practically meaningful excess of within-group reposts on unseen events, relative to a directed activity-preserving reference model with fixed group assignments. The result persists across plausible handling of unmatched references and major accounts. | The excess disappears outside the discovery graph, after preserving activity and popularity, or under plausible endpoint loss. Internal density on the discovery graph is descriptive because clustering was used to find it. |
| **Political echo-chamber pattern in observed engagement.** Selective sharing is associated with independent evidence of political alignment or a narrow content diet. | A structural result paired with independent candidate-relative stance or coded content, plus measures of cross-group and cross-stance amplification. Record who shares a source and which content they share. | Alignment is inferred from the same graph, from the query that retrieved a post, or from reposting alone. Cross-group quotes and replies may reflect criticism or argument, and diverse sources can share the same position. |
| **Restricted exposure or reinforcement of trust and beliefs.** The group excludes or discredits outside voices, or views change through repeated exposure. | Evidence about what users actually see, repeated discourse that explicitly discredits outsiders for a trust claim, or longitudinal evidence of changed views with a credible design for attribution. | Observable shares leave unseen exposure and private beliefs unresolved. Source dominance, a quiet boundary, or a high within-group share gives insufficient evidence for these mechanisms. |

Cinelli et al. motivate testing homophily in interactions alongside selective diffusion. Garimella et al. separate shared content from potential consumption inferred from the follower network. The public ITED-Br files sampled in Step 2 contain no follower graph or feed exposure records. Nguyen’s distinction between missing voices and active discrediting gives a separate question about expressed distrust. Because retweets and mentions can show different cross-group patterns, keep reposts, quotes and replies separate and interpret each in context. The evidence log includes studies that found different patterns by topic or method. [Cinelli et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2023301118) · [Garimella et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3178876.3186139) · [Nguyen, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/epi.2018.32) · [Conover et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v5i1.14126)

## Detection and validation procedure

1. **Build an event table and audit joins.** In a later pilot, start with the two candidate repost contexts in a short October window and build a post-to-author lookup from available public post files. Resolve each sharing event from its post ID, actor ID and reference to the original post and author. Retain the day, context memberships and link status. Deduplicate by event ID while preserving provenance. Keep unmatched target IDs and accounts with uncertain group membership in the coverage counts. In reply and quote contexts, distinguish a verified direct reference from a conversation root or candidate-profile summary. Record timestamp provenance and verify action dates before making chronological claims. [ITED-Br feasibility report](m1-ited-br-feasibility.md)
2. **Discover candidate groups in a training interval.** Use accounts as nodes and observed repost counts as directed weights from sharer to referenced author. Compare directed Louvain and Infomap on the same count-weighted discovery graph. Freeze both partitions before evaluation and rerun Louvain with distinct-pair weights. Retain groups with different profiles, including news media or event-driven groups if they emerge. Track how often an account stays with the same matched group across random seeds and reasonable discovery perturbations such as removing one day. Report consistent membership as a core, unstable assignment as uncertain, and unseen or low-evidence accounts as unassigned. Membership stability summarizes the sensitivity of a partition. It is neither a political label nor a calibrated probability. For large graphs, compare aligned memberships without requiring a dense matrix of all account pairs. [Interian and Rodrigues, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-072X/acf6a4) · [Lancichinetti and Fortunato, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep00336)
3. **Freeze groups and test reposts in a later interval.** Pick the split using coverage and election dates, before calculating the held-out result. Put each action ID into one interval. Keep membership fixed on the evaluation graph. For each group, estimate the proportion of its members’ resolved reposts whose authors are in the same group. Compare it to randomized event graphs that preserve each account’s total observed counts of reposts made and received, and predefine treatment of self-links and parallel events. Preserve day and query stratum where there is enough variation, assigning duplicate contexts to disjoint provenance strata and flagging sparse strata. Report observed and expected shares, the difference in percentage points, the null range, and action and account counts. Report both distinct-pair and equal-sharer summaries to distinguish repeated ties from broad participation. Accounts seen only in the evaluation period stay unassigned and contribute to coverage. A time split tests persistence within the sampled collection and still shares its selection mechanism. [Hobson et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12775) · [Fosdick et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1087175)
4. **Test whether the conclusion depends on the sample.** Compare the two collection contexts where both have adequate coverage. Compare results with and without election day and check whether highly amplified source posts or accounts dominate. To expose the role of missing endpoints, let I and E be known within-group and outside-group events for sharers assigned to that group, and U be observed events without a target assignment. Alongside the resolved share I/(I+E), report bounds from I/(I+E+U) to (I+U)/(I+E+U). The bounds apply to observed events from assigned sharers. Uncollected events, absent accounts and any asymmetric query coverage remain outside them. If unresolved links or sparse data make the conclusion unstable, report the group as inconclusive. 
5. **Add independent evidence where the data support it.** A separately collected and adequately covered quote or reply layer can check how cross-group contact differs from reposting. Compare layers on documented comparable populations and dates. Candidate-profile interactions are evidence about candidate-centered conversations, with a different sampling frame. For a claim about political selectivity, compare independently labeled candidate-relative stance or content sampled outside the signals used for discovery. Publish the coverage of overlap, disagreement and unclassified cases. [Conover et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v5i1.14126) · [Step 2 feasibility report](m1-ited-br-feasibility.md)

The degree-preserving comparison controls for the activity and popularity present in the resolved data. It cannot remove the collection bias or estimate whom users could have seen. Keep thresholds for a meaningful effect, required group size and coverage as pilot design decisions. If formal significance tests are later used for many groups, predefine the comparison set and account for multiple comparisons. [Hobson et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12775)

## Outputs for membership and circulation

| Output | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Group summaries | Assigned accounts, membership stability, unassigned and unresolved shares, held-out internal repost rate, expected rate, their difference, and the missing-endpoint sensitivity result. Give each group the strongest interpretation its evidence supports. |
| Membership records in protected research storage | Account-to-group assignments with stability and coverage, observed role as a producer, sharer or both, and cross-group contacts. A bridge account has observed cross-group actions. That does not establish that it mediated anyone’s exposure. |
| Circulation summaries | Count original posts authored, distinct original post IDs shared, unique sharers and total share events by group. For an origin-by-sharing-group matrix, put the referenced author’s group on the origin axis and the sharer’s group on the other. Include unknown endpoints separately. With hydrated URLs, add destination-domain summaries and report how many URLs could be resolved or normalized. Archive reviewed aggregates and provenance in Git. |

For the held-out matrix, use original-author groups as rows and sharing groups as columns. Alongside counts and unknowns, report each assigned origin's share of a sharing group's resolved events, its expected share, and excess in percentage points. Read both diagonal and off-diagonal cells. Decompose concentration by original post and author, rerunning the comparison on matched eligible events after top-source exclusions. Compare distinct-post repertoires under an account-by-post null that preserves sharer activity and post popularity. Known post IDs remain eligible for that analysis when author joins fail and sharers have frozen assignments. [Report 3 measures and interpretation](reflexive-report-3.md)

A cross-group share establishes that an account in one group reposted a post first authored by an account in another, where both endpoints are resolved. Several groups sharing the same source post can be measured without observing who passed it along. Twitter’s repost references generally point back to the original post, so they cannot establish a complete cascade or its depth. Direct quote or reply chains can be reported as observed, with gaps labeled and conversation roots kept distinct from direct parent links. [Migliorini et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10115-023-01878-7) · [ITED-Br collection paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0316626)
