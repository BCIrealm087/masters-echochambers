# Search and evidence notes for the political-language taxonomy

**M1 · Step 5 supporting notes · 2026-11-24**

These notes document the focused Q3 literature search from the [M1 review protocol](m1-review-protocol.md). The [Step 5 report](m1-political-language-taxonomy.md) draws on this evidence to define the coding units, proposed categories and group comparisons.

## Search record

Seventeen English and Portuguese scholarly web queries were run on 2026-11-24 and the five entries per query were screened by title or snippet. The 85 entries include duplicates across searches. As in Steps 3 and 4, retrieval used a shallower depth per query than the Step 1 starting limit, with targeted publisher and citation lookups. Duplicate records were consolidated by title or DOI, and selected sources were checked for the unit, scope and annotation or analysis procedure. No date limit was applied. Domain restrictions are recorded in the search strings. This was a scoped pass to produce a codebook draft and leaves coverage of rare discourse forms to future work.

## Citation tracing

- From Casadei and Ito, followed the backward reference to Wardle and Derakhshan and read the original report. Casadei and Ito also identify limitations in applying general information-disorder categories to their material. This informed the decision to record claim evidence, its context and speaker behavior individually. [Casadei and Ito](https://doi.org/10.22409/rmc.v18i1.58480) · [Wardle and Derakhshan](https://edoc.coe.int/en/media/7495-information-disorder-toward-an-interdisciplinary-framework-for-research-and-policy-making.html)
- Followed Da San Martino et al. forward to Piskorski et al. The latter extends span-level annotation across six European languages and discusses the earlier news corpus directly. Its examples motivate testing labels rather than transferring an entire inventory to election tweets. [Da San Martino et al.](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1565/) · [Piskorski et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.169/)
- Retained two sources for claim retrieval and matching. Hale et al. give a direct 2022 Brazilian case, and Shaar et al. describe the separate task of retrieving a previously checked claim. Their samples answer different questions and neither provides a coverage estimate for the current archive. [Hale et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/ijpor/edae032) · [Shaar et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.332/)

## Evidence extracted

| Source | Population, coding or measure | What transfers and what remains uncertain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Furlani and Mitozo](https://doi.org/10.26694/2317-3254.rcp.v12i2.6000) | Content analysis of 652 posts from Bolsonaro's account in the 2022 official campaign, with themes, strategies and orientation. | A Portuguese campaign-codebook example. An official campaign account is a different population from group members and the study covers one candidate. |
| [Ferreira et al.](https://doi.org/10.26694/2317-3254.rcp.v13i2.6933) | Twitter posts about the 2022 electoral system from 25 September to 8 October. Three coders examined the most-reposted 50 posts from each selected political cluster. | Demonstrates that emotion and discourse can be coded after grouping. Selected posts describe salient discourse in one topic. Group prevalence requires a different sample. |
| [Casadei and Ito, 2024](https://doi.org/10.22409/rmc.v18i1.58480) | Critical discourse analysis of selected Brazilian 2022 misinformation about election integrity, spanning Twitter, Telegram and other sources. Identifies ways material borrows perceived credibility. | Motivates recording testimony, statistics and authority cues. Observing a cue alone leaves the assertion's truth and the audience's interpretation open. |
| [Analysis of Bolsonaro's Twitter content](https://www.scielo.br/j/ccrh/a/RjnW4vKFx64hxmLBkhc8Ftr/?lang=pt) | Codes 245 posts in August and 275 in October 2022 from one candidate account. Reports that categories can overlap in a post. | Supports multi-label coding. Some source categories combine rhetorical, issue and disinformation judgments, which need distinct evidence for this thesis. Its candidate-specific frequencies have limited transfer. |
| [Mohammad et al., 2016](https://aclanthology.org/S16-2021/) | Target-based stance in English tweets with separate sentiment annotation. | Establishes the post–target relation as a useful unit and motivates checking what the sentiment is directed toward. Local language cues need Brazilian Portuguese interpretation. |
| [UstanceBR](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10579-025-09896-3) · [Brazilian political-elite stance corpus](https://aclanthology.org/2025.nlp4dh-1.32/) | Brazilian Portuguese datasets for target-based stance. The political-elite corpus separately codes relevance, stance and sentiment on vaccination posts. | Provides local-language examples of category boundaries and coder checks. The populations, dates or political targets differ from the thesis question. |
| [Da San Martino et al., 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1565/) · [Piskorski et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.169/) | Fine-grained news datasets with techniques anchored in spans. The later study applies related methods to six European languages. | Use span grounding and allow co-occurring tactics. The news genre and languages differ from short Brazilian Portuguese election posts. |
| [Hale et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/ijpor/edae032) | Compares submitted and collected claims on WhatsApp, Twitter and Kwai around the 2022 Brazilian election. Notes limited overlap and differences in the form and detail of shared claims across settings. | A claim match needs attention to the actual proposition and qualifiers. The tiplines and platform samples capture selected material, so their topical frequencies cannot be mapped directly onto ITED-Br groups. |
| [Shaar et al., 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.332/) | Models retrieval of previously fact-checked claims using examples paired by fact-checkers or related datasets. | Supports matching as a separate retrieval task with relevance checks. Retrieval supplies candidates for evidence, and independent checking may still be needed. |
| [TSE 2022 Fato ou Boato archive](https://www.justicaeleitoral.jus.br/fato-ou-boato/checagens/2022) | Dated historical election fact-checks on specific allegations. | Source for evidence links and for locating relevant records. The category of an allegation in the archive does not supply a verdict for other versions or unmatched claims. |
| [Wardle and Derakhshan, 2017](https://edoc.coe.int/en/media/7495-information-disorder-toward-an-interdisciplinary-framework-for-research-and-policy-making.html) | Conceptual framework distinguishing types of information disorder and agent, message and interpreter. | Motivates keeping factual status, act of sharing and evidence of intent distinct. The framework cannot establish intent for a given ITED-Br account. |
| [Pacheco et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v15i1.18075) | Unsupervised coordination networks based on shared behavioral traces, illustrated in case studies. | Coordination requires evidence outside the text of a single post. Popularity, shared news events and group formation can also produce similarity. |
| [Artstein and Poesio, 2008](https://aclanthology.org/J08-4004/) | Review of agreement measures for annotation, consulted in Step 1. | Supports later independent annotations and reporting disagreement by code. Agreement supplements evidence that a category measures the intended construct. |
